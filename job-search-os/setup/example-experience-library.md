## Story Bank

### Story 1: The Onboarding Overhaul
- **Situation:** Vantage's self-serve signup-to-activation rate was stuck at 18% for three quarters. The growth team didn't exist yet — the CEO asked me to figure out why users were signing up and then disappearing.
- **Task:** Diagnose the activation bottleneck, propose a solution, and ship it within one quarter. Success meant moving 7-day activation above 25%.
- **Action:** I started by pulling Mixpanel data to map the full signup-to-activation funnel. Found that 62% of users dropped off at "connect your first data source" — the third step. Ran 15 user interviews in two weeks: users said the step felt like a commitment before they'd seen any value. I proposed inverting the flow: show a pre-built demo dashboard first, then ask for data connection. Worked with 1 designer and 3 engineers for 6 weeks to ship the new flow. We A/B tested against the original with a 50/50 split over 3 weeks.
- **Result:** Activation jumped from 18% to 31%. The variant was statistically significant at p<0.01 after 11 days. Over two quarters, this added $2.1M ARR from self-serve alone. The CEO cited it in the board deck as the key product win of the half.
- **Tags:** accomplishment, growth-metrics, data-driven, customer-insight

### Story 2: The Salesforce Integration Bet
- **Situation:** At Vantage, our integrations marketplace had Slack, HubSpot, and Jira — but our #1 enterprise request for 9 months was Salesforce. Sales was losing deals because prospects saw no Salesforce integration and walked. But Salesforce's API is notoriously complex, and engineering leadership estimated 16 weeks.
- **Task:** Get the Salesforce integration shipped fast enough to save Q3 pipeline, without overcommitting the team or shipping something fragile.
- **Action:** I interviewed 28 existing customer accounts to understand exactly which Salesforce objects they needed synced. Found that 80% of the value was in bi-directional sync of Contacts and Opportunities — not the full Salesforce schema. Scoped a "Phase 1" covering just those two objects with a simpler sync architecture. Negotiated with the eng lead to pull 4 engineers for a focused 9-week sprint instead of the full 16-week estimate. I wrote the API contract spec myself (CS background helped), which cut the scoping phase from 2 weeks to 3 days. Ran weekly check-ins with 5 beta customers during development.
- **Result:** Shipped in 9 weeks. 340 accounts adopted within 90 days. Sales attributed 3 closed deals ($420K ACV) directly to the integration being available. Phase 2 (full schema sync) shipped 4 months later with learnings from Phase 1.
- **Tags:** technical-challenge, prioritization, stakeholder-management, say-no
- **Best for:** "Tell me about a time you had to make a tradeoff," "Tell me about a time you shipped something fast," "Tell me about a time you said no to stakeholders"

### Story 3: The Pricing Restructure Pushback
- **Situation:** Vantage had a 2-tier pricing model (Free and Pro) that hadn't changed in 2 years. Data showed that 34% of Pro users were using less than 20% of Pro features, while 12% of Free users were power users who'd never convert because the jump to Pro was too steep. I proposed adding a middle tier.
- **Task:** Design a new 3-tier structure, build the business case, get executive buy-in, and ship it without churning existing customers.
- **Action:** Built a revenue model in Google Sheets showing the expected impact of a new "Growth" tier priced between Free and Pro. Modeled 3 scenarios (conservative, base, optimistic). The CFO pushed back hard — she was worried about Pro downgrades cannibalizing revenue. I addressed this by analyzing 6 months of usage data showing that the users who'd shift to Growth were already at high churn risk on Pro. Presented to CEO, CFO, and VP Sales in a 30-minute session. Got conditional approval: ship to new signups first, migrate existing users after 90 days of data. Worked with design on the new pricing page, eng on the billing system changes, and marketing on the rollout messaging.
- **Result:** 22% increase in average contract value across new signups. Pro downgrade rate was 4% — well below the CFO's 15% fear threshold. After 90 days, migrated existing users. Net revenue impact: +$1.4M ARR in the first year. The CFO became the biggest advocate for the new model.
- **Tags:** stakeholder-management, data-driven, conflict, strategy
- **Best for:** "Tell me about a time you dealt with pushback," "Tell me about a time you influenced without authority," "Tell me about a strategic decision you made"

### Story 4: The Enterprise Feature That Almost Wasn't
- **Situation:** At Clearpath Analytics, our enterprise pipeline was stuck. We had 6 enterprise prospects in late-stage negotiations, but 4 of them had flagged the lack of role-based access controls (RBAC) as a blocker. Our engineering team was small (11 engineers total) and already committed to the dashboard builder v2 roadmap.
- **Task:** Figure out how to ship RBAC without derailing the dashboard v2 timeline, which was our primary OKR.
- **Action:** I mapped the RBAC requirements from all 4 enterprise prospects and found significant overlap — they all needed the same 3 permission levels. I proposed a phased approach: ship a "simple RBAC" (3 roles, workspace-level permissions) in 4 weeks as a parallel workstream with 2 engineers, then iterate to granular permissions post-launch. The VP of Engineering was skeptical about splitting the team. I built a one-page analysis showing the $1.8M deal value at risk vs. the 2-week dashboard delay, and presented it at the weekly leadership sync. Got approval. Personally wrote the product spec in 2 days, held daily 15-minute standups with the 2 engineers, and ran weekly demos with the enterprise prospects to keep them engaged.
- **Result:** Shipped simple RBAC in 3.5 weeks. All 4 enterprise deals closed within 60 days, totaling $1.8M. Dashboard v2 shipped only 1 week late. The experience taught me that perfect prioritization isn't about saying no — it's about finding the right scope.
- **Tags:** prioritization, leadership, ambiguity, cross-functional
- **Best for:** "Tell me about a time you had to prioritize competing demands," "Tell me about a time you operated under ambiguity," "Tell me about a time you showed leadership without a leadership title"

### Story 5: The Experiment That Failed
- **Situation:** After the onboarding success, I was confident that adding a gamification layer (progress bar, badges, streaks) would push activation even higher. The team had momentum, and I pushed to ship it in the next sprint without doing the same level of customer research I'd done for the onboarding overhaul.
- **Task:** Ship the gamification experiment and increase activation from 31% to 35%+.
- **Action:** Designed the gamification system with the designer in 1 week. Shipped progress bars, completion badges, and a 7-day streak tracker in 3 weeks. A/B tested with a 50/50 split. After 2 weeks, activation in the variant was 29.8% — actually lower than control (31.2%). I dug into the data and found that the gamification elements were distracting users during the critical "connect data source" step. The progress bar made users feel like setup was a chore rather than a path to value. I killed the experiment, wrote a detailed post-mortem, and shared it with the full product org.
- **Result:** No activation improvement — actually a slight regression. But the post-mortem surfaced a key insight: our users are data professionals who respond to value demonstration, not gamification. This reframed our entire growth strategy toward showing ROI faster rather than making the product "stickier." Three subsequent experiments based on this insight all won. I now require customer research validation before any experiment that changes user psychology, not just UI.
- **Tags:** failure, learning, self-awareness, data-driven
- **Best for:** "Tell me about a time you failed," "Tell me about a time you were wrong," "Tell me about a time you learned from a mistake"
