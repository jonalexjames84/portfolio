# Example: Completed Career Plan

> This is a fictional example showing what a fully filled career plan looks like. Your career plan should have similar depth and specificity.
> **Do not copy this content.** Use it as a reference for structure and level of detail.

---

## Level Preference (rank 1-3)

1. Senior PM at a growth-stage or late-stage tech company (Series C+ or public) — I want ownership of a meaningful product area, not a feature team. 5 years of experience puts me solidly in Senior PM territory.
2. PM Lead / Group PM at a Series B-C startup — willing to take a slightly smaller company if I get to manage 1-2 PMs and own a full product line. This accelerates the path to Director.
3. Senior PM at a Big Tech company (Google, Meta, Microsoft) — would take this for the brand signal and comp, but it's third because I prefer faster-moving environments and broader scope.

## Industry Preference

B2B SaaS first, always. I know this space deeply after 5 years (analytics, integrations, self-serve growth). Within B2B SaaS, my priority order:

1. **AI/ML-powered products** — companies building AI into core workflows, not just tacking on a chatbot. Think Notion AI, Figma AI, Glean, Writer. I want to be at the intersection of AI and product, not doing pure ML infrastructure.
2. **Developer tools and platforms** — Vercel, Supabase, Retool, Datadog. My CS background and API experience make me a strong fit. These companies value PMs who can talk to engineers.
3. **Data and analytics** — my Clearpath Analytics experience maps directly. Hex, Sigma, Monte Carlo, dbt.

Not interested in: adtech, gaming, crypto, hardware, or consumer social. Open to fintech if the product is genuinely good (Stripe, Ramp, Mercury — yes. Generic neobanks — no).

## Function / Role Type

Product Manager on a growth or core product team. Specific preferences:

- **Growth PM** is my sweet spot. I've built experimentation programs, redesigned onboarding funnels, restructured pricing. This is where I'm differentiated.
- **Core product / 0-to-1** as a close second. The Salesforce integration and RBAC stories show I can ship from scratch.
- **Platform PM** only if it's a developer-facing product (APIs, integrations). I don't want to own internal platform infrastructure.
- **Not interested in:** pure strategy/chief-of-staff PM roles, program management disguised as PM, or PMM-adjacent roles.

## Remote vs In-Person

Hybrid preferred, 2-3 days per week in office. Based in SF, willing to commute to South Bay.

- **SF or Peninsula:** Ideal. No relocation needed.
- **Fully remote:** Acceptable for the right company (Series C+, strong remote culture, not a "remote as afterthought" setup). Would need to see that other senior PMs are also remote.
- **NYC:** Would relocate only for a clear top-choice opportunity (Notion, for example). Would need relocation support and a compelling reason over equivalent SF roles.
- **Not open to:** Seattle, Austin, or other locations unless fully remote.

## Target Companies Preference

I want companies building products that knowledge workers use every day, where the PM role involves real product decisions (not just project management). Specifically:

- Series C through public. I want some stability and a real PM org, but not so large that I'm a cog. 200-5,000 employees is the sweet spot.
- Strong product culture — the PM is respected, not order-taking from founders or sales.
- Revenue above $30M ARR. I've done the early-stage scrappiness; now I want to operate at scale.
- Companies where my growth PM + technical background is a differentiator, not table stakes.

Top-tier targets: Notion, Figma, Vercel, Retool, Datadog, Glean, Writer, Hex.
Strong fits: Supabase, Monte Carlo, LaunchDarkly, Amplitude, dbt Labs, Sigma Computing, Weights & Biases.
Stretch targets (Big Tech): Google (Workspace or Cloud), Stripe, Atlassian.

## Addressing-Weaknesses Analysis

### Weakness 1: No Big Tech brand on resume
**The gap:** Both Clearpath Analytics and Vantage are respected in B2B SaaS circles but are not household names. Recruiters at larger companies may filter me out early if they're looking for Google/Meta/Amazon alumni.

**How to address:**
- Lead with metrics, not company names. "$2.1M ARR from a single onboarding experiment" catches attention regardless of where it happened.
- Resume format: put the impact bullets first, company context second. Force the reader to engage with results before judging the brand.
- In "tell me about yourself," position the startup experience as a strength: "I've owned end-to-end product at companies where there's no brand to hide behind — every product decision shows up directly in revenue."
- For Big Tech applications specifically: emphasize the experimentation rigor (17 A/B tests, statistical significance, instrumentation) and cross-functional scale (2 squads, 8 engineers). This signals I can operate in their environment.
- Work products are the equalizer. A strong 1-pager about their product proves PM thinking more than a brand name ever could.

### Weakness 2: No people management experience (IC only)
**The gap:** Some Senior PM and all Group PM / Lead PM roles ask for direct reports. I've mentored 2 APMs but never had formal reporting lines. This limits me for roles that explicitly require management experience.

**How to address:**
- Be honest and precise: "I've mentored 2 APMs through their first year (both promoted within 14 months) but haven't had direct reports. I've managed cross-functional teams of 10+ and run quarterly planning. The gap is specifically the performance review and career development piece."
- For roles that require management: acknowledge the gap directly in the cover letter. Frame it as a known growth area, not a secret weakness. Hiring managers respect self-awareness.
- Position the mentorship data: "Both APMs I mentored were promoted within 14 months" is a concrete signal of people development ability.
- Target roles where strong IC with mentorship experience is sufficient (many Senior PM roles). Don't waste applications on Director roles that need 3+ years of management.
- In interviews, use the RBAC story (Story 4) to demonstrate leadership without formal authority — the most transferable management skill.

## Dream Offer

**Company:** Notion
**Role:** Senior Product Manager, Growth
**Comp target:** $320K+ total (base $190K + equity $110K+ + bonus)
**Why this specifically:** Notion is the product I use most and understand most deeply. Their growth challenges (activation, expansion, enterprise adoption) map exactly to what I've done at Vantage. Their product culture is legendary — PMs have real ownership. SF-based with strong hybrid culture. And their AI features are creating a new growth surface that needs a PM who understands both product-led growth and technical implementation.
**What would make me say no:** Below $280K total comp. A role scoped to a single feature (e.g., "PM for Notion Calendar" only) rather than a growth surface. Fully in-office 5 days a week. Or if the team/manager felt like a bad fit during interviews — culture matters more than brand.
