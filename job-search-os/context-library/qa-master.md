### "What is your greatest weakness?"
I can get too deep into building mode and lose sight of the broader stakeholder picture. At Pottery Friends, I spent weeks refining features before pulling back and running user interviews that told me to cut half of what I'd built. The lesson stuck: I now force myself to validate before I invest. I set explicit "check the data" milestones before committing to any build.

### "What is your biggest failure?"
At Pottery Friends, I designed an ambitious 40-quest onboarding flow because I was convinced users needed guided progression. The data showed the opposite — users were dropping off because they wanted to explore, not follow a script. I had to cut it to 8 quests and rethink the entire home screen. The failure taught me to prototype the riskiest assumption first, not the most exciting feature.

### "Where do you see yourself in 3 years?"
I want to be the PM who's shipped a product that meaningfully changes how people use AI in their daily work. In 3 years, I see myself leading a product area — either as a senior IC who's become the go-to person for a critical surface, or growing into a team lead who's building the PM practice. The specifics depend on where the company goes, but the constant is: I want to be building, not just managing process.

### "Tell me about yourself" (2 min max)
I'm a PM with 15 years shipping products — started at Zynga during FarmVille's peak, scaled a game to $50M at Jam City, was the first PM at Genies where I built the creator ecosystem from scratch. Most recently I was at Treasure DAO building developer platforms and marketplaces.

When I was laid off last year, I didn't just job search — I built. I co-founded a game studio, and I built Pottery Friends, a mobile app for craft studio communities, completely solo using AI-native development with Claude Code. 150 beta members, 15 repos, 59 database migrations. I also started consulting as a fractional AI PM, building prototypes that got used in investor meetings.

The through-line is: I'm a PM who builds. I don't just write specs and hand them off — I ship production software. That's rare, and it means I can move faster, have deeper technical conversations, and make better prioritization decisions because I know what things actually cost to build.
